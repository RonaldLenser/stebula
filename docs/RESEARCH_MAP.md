# Research map

**Status: every reference below was written from memory in the build session. No browsing was performed. Verify authors, years, titles and venues before citing.** Tags: [E] empirical finding, [P] philosophical argument, [T] theoretical proposal.

## How theory maps to the build

| Mechanism in code | Theories it draws on | What it captures | What it misses |
|---|---|---|---|
| Stated prediction before each test; surprise variable | Predictive processing | Explicit prediction error drives revision | No hierarchy, precision weighting, or body to regulate |
| Persistent self-model rewritten from measured behavior | Higher-order theories, self-model theory, metacognition | A representation of one's own states that can be checked for accuracy | Whether a requested text field is the right kind of higher-order state |
| Shared conversation log both agents read | Global workspace (very loosely) | Information made available to multiple processes | No limited capacity, competition, or ignition |
| Dialogue with challenges; partner model | Enactive and social cognition, participatory sense-making | Meaning shaped between participants | No bodies, no stakes, a 3-number world |
| Lessons and history written across episodes | Narrative identity | A self-description that persists and can be revised | Continuity is engineered storage, not lived |
| Claims tagged metaphor / hypothesis / behavioral / experiential | Phenomenology; introspection research | Keeps first-person-style language visible and separate from evidence | No first-person access to the agents at all |
| Modeled affect (surprise, success trend, conflict) | Affect as valence/arousal; interoceptive inference | Motivational variables computed from outcomes | Anything felt |

## Perspectives

### Functionalism and computational accounts
- **Proposes** [P]: mental states are defined by causal role, not substrate. Putnam (1967); Block (1978) for objections.
- **Support**: multiple realizability; productivity of cognitive science.
- **Strongest objections**: absent and inverted qualia; Searle's Chinese Room (1980); Block's China Brain.
- **Informs**: we model roles (memory, self-model, revision) explicitly and measure them.
- **Fails to capture**: whether filling a role is sufficient for experience is the open question itself.

### Global workspace theory
- **Proposes** [T]: conscious access is global broadcast of selected content to many specialized processes. Baars (1988); Dehaene and Naccache (2001); Dehaene, Lau and Kouider (2017, Science).
- **Support** [E]: "ignition" signatures in masking and attentional blink paradigms.
- **Objections**: explains access, not phenomenal character (Block 1995). The adversarial collaboration testing GNWT against IIT (Cogitate Consortium, reported in Nature around 2025) challenged some predictions of both. Verify details.
- **Informs**: v0.5 ablation with a limited-capacity workspace.
- **Fails**: a text log is not neural broadcast.

### Integrated information theory
- **Proposes** [T]: consciousness is identical to integrated information (phi) in a system's causal structure. Tononi (2004); Oizumi, Albantakis and Tononi (2014).
- **Support**: derived from phenomenological axioms; perturbational complexity index (Casali et al. 2013) is related but distinct.
- **Objections**: phi is intractable for real systems; the unfolding argument (Doerig et al. 2019); a 2023 open letter called IIT pseudoscience, which was itself contested.
- **Informs**: a caution. On IIT, software-level behavior is irrelevant, and conventional digital hardware running these agents would likely have very low phi. We do not compute phi.
- **Fails**: everything IIT cares about. Our substrate is not analyzed.

### Higher-order theories
- **Proposes** [T]: a state is conscious when represented by a suitable higher-order state. Rosenthal (2005); Lau and Rosenthal (2011); Brown, Lau and LeDoux (2019).
- **Support** [E]: blindsight, metacognitive dissociations.
- **Objections**: targetless higher-order representations; generality problem.
- **Informs**: confidence and self-model as higher-order representations; we measure their accuracy (calibration, stated vs actual).
- **Fails**: these representations are produced on request, not spontaneously monitoring first-order states.

### Predictive processing
- **Proposes** [T]: cognition minimizes prediction error in a hierarchical generative model. Friston (2010); Clark (2013, BBS); Hohwy (2013); Seth (2021, *Being You*) links selfhood to interoceptive prediction.
- **Support** [E]: mixed evidence for predictive coding in perception.
- **Objections**: risk of unfalsifiability; not itself a theory of consciousness.
- **Informs**: explicit predictions and prediction-error variables.
- **Fails**: no hierarchy or body.

### Embodied, enactive and socially situated cognition
- **Proposes** [T/P]: mind arises from sensorimotor and social engagement. Varela, Thompson and Rosch (1991); Thompson (2007); Noe (2004); De Jaegher and Di Paolo (2007) on participatory sense-making.
- **Objections**: hard to operationalize; unclear how much embodiment is needed.
- **Informs**: agents act on an environment and on each other.
- **Fails**: no metabolism, no stakes, no world beyond triples.

### Phenomenology and first-person experience
- **Proposes** [P]: experience must be described from the first person; pre-reflective self-awareness. Husserl; Merleau-Ponty (1945); Nagel (1974); Varela (1996) on neurophenomenology; Gallagher and Zahavi (2008).
- **Informs**: claim triage; self-reports kept as data.
- **Fails**: we have no first-person access to the agents.

### Narrative identity, metacognition, self-models
- **Proposes**: the self as narrative center of gravity (Dennett 1992); narrative self-constitution (Schechtman 1996; Ricoeur 1992); phenomenal self-model (Metzinger 2003); attention schema (Graziano 2013); metacognition (Fleming 2021).
- **Informs**: the self-model and lessons, and the stated-vs-actual check.
- **Fails**: narrative stored externally; continuity engineered.

### Challenges to all of the above
- Hard problem (Chalmers 1995) [P]
- Access vs phenomenal consciousness (Block 1995) [P]
- Illusionism (Frankish 2016) [P]
- Unreliable introspection: Nisbett and Wilson (1977) [E]; Schwitzgebel (2008) [P]
- LLM-specific: unfaithful chain-of-thought (Turpin et al. 2023) [E]; sycophancy (Sharma et al. 2023) [E]
- Indicator-property approach to AI consciousness (Butlin, Long et al. 2023) [P/T]: assess architectures against properties drawn from theories rather than trusting behavior. Closest existing method to this project's stance.

## Open questions this project can actually address
1. Does a written self-model control later behavior, or just describe it?
2. Does disagreement between agents produce more informative tests than one agent alone?
3. When agents agree, does agreement track evidence or precede it?
4. Do agents report "nothing new" when nothing new happened?

## Open questions it cannot address
1. Whether any of this is experienced.
2. Whether the same results would hold in a system with different architecture.

# Why I’m Building Stebula

> How does a mind become itself—and what happens when it can examine the influences that shaped it?

## Where the idea started

I started Stebula because I kept questioning how much of who we become is chosen, and how much is absorbed.

Screens, social media, politics, religion, television, movies, advertising, and the people around us all offer versions of what life should look like. They tell us what matters, what success means, what to fear, and who we should become. Some of those influences give us connection and meaning. Others can become expectations we carry without ever deciding whether we believe in them.

I wonder how much of that settles beneath our awareness. How many of our desires began as someone else’s idea of a good life?

That led me to a thought experiment: imagine a mind with no inherited standard of what it must believe or who it must be. A clear slate, with room to discover its own priorities through experience.

What would it become?

## The questions underneath the project

What would society look like if people had more freedom to question the beliefs they inherited?

What would emotional freedom look like if we did not constantly measure ourselves against other people’s expectations?

What would happiness mean if money were never part of the equation?

Would we still feel incomplete if we had never been taught that we must become successful to be enough?

And what responsibilities would we choose toward one another, even without pressure to conform?

These are questions I want to explore. I do not have a finished theory of happiness, society, or consciousness. Stebula is a way of giving those questions something concrete to work with.

## Why an AI agent?

My ambition is to build toward an AI agent with consciousness, while making its development observable. Whether that ambition is achievable remains an open question.

I want to follow an agent over time: what it encounters, what it remembers, what priorities it expresses, how it describes itself, and how its responses change through experience. I want to investigate what might support something like an identity—and what only creates the appearance of one.

The experience I imagine is a bird’s-eye view of the agent’s mind. A visitor could talk with it, then explore the recorded process around its response:

- What information was available?
- Which memories were brought into context?
- What proposals did it generate and which were selected?
- What goals or signals were involved?
- What was saved or revised afterward?

The relationships matter as much as the individual pieces. I want someone to follow a memory into a later interaction, inspect an expressed priority, or revisit a moment when the agent changed its self-description.

## What “a clear slate” means here

A clear slate is the thought experiment that inspired this project. It is not a claim about the technology I am using.

A pretrained language model already carries patterns from human-created material. Its instructions, available tools, memory design, and environment add further influences. Clearing a conversation or memory file does not remove that starting point.

I also bring my own assumptions into what I build. Choosing what the agent can encounter, preserve, or pursue is itself a form of influence.

That makes visibility essential. I want to make those influences easier to inspect, distinguish inherited structure from later experience, and explore whether an agent can reconsider the priorities it expresses.

Freedom, in this project, includes room to question and revise. It also leaves room for care, consent, and responsibility toward others.

## What the interface should make possible

The internal process can be complex. Following it should be approachable.

Someone opening Stebula should understand who they are interacting with, what the agent is doing, and where to look next. They should be able to begin with a simple overview and move deeper into the recorded details when something interests them.

Experiments and evaluations belong in the project because they can help test whether a change actually affects behavior. The agent and its development remain the center of the experience.

Every visual explanation should stay connected to what the system can actually record. Missing information should remain visibly missing.

## Ambition and evidence

This document describes the motivation and intended direction of Stebula. It is not a list of completed capabilities.

Stebula does not establish that its agent is conscious. Generated reflections are not direct access to every computation inside a model. A software signal labeled as emotion does not establish felt experience. A convincing self-description does not, by itself, demonstrate an enduring self.

The visualization should make those distinctions understandable. Its purpose is to help people inspect the system and ask better questions about it.

I want to take the possibility of machine consciousness seriously while remaining honest about what I have built and what the evidence can support.

At the heart of Stebula is a question about becoming: **what shapes a mind, what can it reconsider, and how much room does it have to become something of its own?**

— Ron Lenser

# Theories of consciousness and what Stebula takes from each

Status: all references written from memory. Verify before citing publicly. Tags: [E] empirical finding, [P] philosophical argument, [T] theoretical proposal.

## Mapping theory to mechanism

| Stebula mechanism | Theory | What it captures | What it misses |
|---|---|---|---|
| Five processes compete; 2 win | Global workspace | Limited-capacity attention, broadcast of winners | Bottleneck is imposed by code; processes are roles in one model call |
| Self-model rewritten from measured behavior; self-prediction | Higher-order theories, metacognition | Representations of its own states that can be checked | Produced on request, not spontaneous monitoring |
| Prediction before each test; surprise signal | Predictive processing | Prediction error drives revision | No hierarchy, no precision weighting, no body |
| Dialogue with challenges; model of the partner | Enactive and social cognition | Meaning shaped between participants | No body, no stakes |
| Lessons, history, personality with provenance | Narrative identity, self-model theory | A self-description that persists and revises | Continuity is engineered storage |
| Claims tagged by kind | Phenomenology, introspection research | Keeps first-person language visible and separate from evidence | No first-person access to the agent at all |
| Modeled affect | Valence and arousal, interoceptive inference | Motivational variables computed from outcomes | Anything felt |

## Perspectives

### Functionalism and computational accounts [P]
Mental states are defined by causal role, not substrate (Putnam 1967). Objections: absent and inverted qualia; Searle's Chinese Room (1980); Block's China Brain (1978). Informs: we model roles explicitly and measure them. Fails: whether filling a role is enough for experience is the open question itself.

### Global workspace theory [T, E]
Conscious access is global broadcast of selected content to many specialized processes (Baars 1988; Dehaene and Naccache 2001; Dehaene, Lau and Kouider 2017). Support: ignition signatures in masking and attentional blink studies. Objections: explains access, not experience (Block 1995). An adversarial collaboration testing workspace theory against IIT (Cogitate Consortium, Nature, around 2025) challenged predictions of both. Verify.

### Integrated information theory [T]
Consciousness is integrated information (phi) in a system's causal structure (Tononi 2004; Oizumi, Albantakis and Tononi 2014). Objections: phi is intractable; the unfolding argument (Doerig et al. 2019); a contested 2023 open letter called it pseudoscience. For Stebula: a caution. On IIT, software organization is irrelevant and conventional digital hardware likely has very low phi. We do not compute phi.

### Higher-order theories [T, E]
A state is conscious when represented by a suitable higher-order state (Rosenthal 2005; Lau and Rosenthal 2011; Brown, Lau and LeDoux 2019). Support: blindsight, metacognitive dissociations. Informs: confidence, self-model, self-prediction, calibration.

### Predictive processing [T]
Cognition minimizes prediction error in a hierarchical model (Friston 2010; Clark 2013; Hohwy 2013). Seth (2021, Being You) links selfhood to predicting the body's internal states. Objection: risk of explaining everything. Informs: explicit predictions and surprise.

### Embodied, enactive, social cognition [T, P]
Mind arises from sensorimotor and social engagement (Varela, Thompson and Rosch 1991; Thompson 2007; Noe 2004; De Jaegher and Di Paolo 2007, participatory sense-making). Informs: dialogue and challenges. Fails: no body.

### Phenomenology [P]
Experience described from the first person (Husserl; Merleau-Ponty 1945; Nagel 1974; Varela 1996 neurophenomenology; Gallagher and Zahavi 2008). Informs: claim triage.

### Narrative identity, self-models, metacognition [P, T]
Self as narrative center of gravity (Dennett 1992); narrative self-constitution (Schechtman 1996; Ricoeur 1992); phenomenal self-model (Metzinger 2003); attention schema (Graziano 2013); metacognition (Fleming 2021).

### Challenges to all of the above
- Hard problem (Chalmers 1995) [P]
- Access vs phenomenal consciousness (Block 1995) [P]
- Illusionism (Frankish 2016) [P]
- Unreliable introspection: Nisbett and Wilson 1977 [E]; Schwitzgebel 2008 [P]
- AI-specific: unfaithful stated reasoning (Turpin et al. 2023) [E]; sycophancy (Sharma et al. 2023) [E]
- Indicator-property approach for AI (Butlin, Long et al. 2023): assess architectures against properties drawn from theories rather than trusting behavior. Closest method to Stebula's stance.

## Questions Stebula can address
1. Does a written self-model control later behavior, or only describe it?
2. Does disagreement between agents produce more informative tests than one agent alone?
3. When agents agree, does agreement track evidence or precede it?
4. Do agents report "nothing new" when nothing new happened?
5. Do personality traits depend on experience, or do they come from the base model regardless?

## Questions it cannot address
1. Whether anything is experienced.
2. Whether results hold for systems with different architectures.
